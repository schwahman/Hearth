Server Admin Guide

Everything server owners and moderators can do, and how to set up a server that runs smoothly. Most of it lives behind the ⚙ gear next to your server's name.

On this page 1. Quick setup checklist 2. Server settings 3. Channels 4. Roles and permissions 5. Managing members 6. Reports 7. Audit log 8. Moderator commands 9. Ownership and deleting 10. Your responsibilities 11. Hearth admins (site operators) 12. Moving a server from Discord

1. Quick setup checklist

A setup that works well for most communities:

  1. Create a "Mod" role (⚙ → Roles → Create role) with: Timeout members and handle reports, Delete others' messages, Kick members, View audit log. Add Ban members only for people you trust most. Give it to your moderators.
  2. Make a read-only #rules channel: create a text channel, tick Read-only, and tick only the Mod role. Post your rules there.
  3. Make a private #staff channel: tick Private and the Mod role. Only mods will see it.
  4. Turn on slowmode in busy channels (for example 5 seconds) to calm spam.
  5. Mark adult channels 18+ if you have any. Never post adult content anywhere else.
  6. Invite people with ⚙ → Invite or /invite.

3. Channels

  • Create: tap + next to "Text channels" or "Voice channels", or next to a category to create a channel inside it. Edit: hover a channel and tap its ⚙.
  • Categories: tap + New category at the bottom of the channel list. Put a channel in a category from the channel's ⚙. Tap a category name to fold it up. Deleting a category keeps its channels.
  • Topic: a short line shown at the top of a text channel.
  • Slowmode: each person can post once every 5 seconds up to once an hour. Moderators aren't limited. Quick version: /slowmode 10 or /slowmode off.
  • Who can view it:
    • Everyone (13+): the default.
    • 18+ only: for adult content. Hidden from anyone under 18, and people see a warning before entering.
    • 21+ only: for alcohol, cannabis or gambling content where the law requires it.
  • Private channel: tick it and only roles you allow (plus the owner and Administrators) can see the channel. Everyone else won't even know it exists.
  • Permissions per role (like Discord): pick a role on the left (or + Add a role), then set each permission to ✓ Allow, ✕ Deny, or / (use what the role normally has).
    • Read-only channel (#rules, #announcements): @everyone → Send messages ✕; your Mods role → Send messages ✓.
    • Staff-only channel: tick Private channel, then Staff role → View channel ✓.
    • How it adds up: a Deny on @everyone is overridden by an Allow on any role the person has. Owners and Administrators always get in.
  • Delete: the channel's ⚙ → Delete. You'll type its name to confirm. Messages disappear for everyone right away (see section 9 for how deletion works).

4. Roles and permissions

How roles work

  • @everyone is the base role every member has. Its permissions are what a brand-new member can do.
  • Other roles add permissions. A member gets everything from all their roles combined.
  • Order matters. Roles higher in the list outrank lower ones. Use ▲ ▼ to reorder. You can only edit, assign or move roles below your own highest role, and only act (timeout, kick, ban) on members whose highest role is below yours. The owner outranks everyone.
  • You can't give a role a permission you don't have yourself.
  • A role's color colors its members' names (the highest colored role wins).
  • Assign roles: ⚙ → Members, then tap the role chips next to a person. Highlighted means they have it.

5. Managing members

Open ⚙ → Members, or tap a person in the member list.

  • Timeout (10 minutes to 1 week): they can still read but can't post, react, or use voice. They're removed from voice right away. End a timeout early with End timeout. Timed-out members show ⏳.
  • Kick: removes them; they can come back with a new invite.
  • Ban: removes them and blocks rejoining. Undo it in ⚙ → Bans.
  • Every action asks for a reason, which is saved in the audit log. Good reasons help your team stay consistent.

6. Reports

  • When a member reports a message or person in your server, it appears in ⚙ → Reports for anyone with Timeout members and handle reports, and you get a notice.
  • Each report shows the category, the reporter's note, and a preserved copy of the reported message, even if it was deleted since.
  • Actions: Dismiss, Delete message, Timeout 1h, Kick, Ban, with an optional note. Closed reports are kept under Show closed.
  • Reporters are anonymous to the person they reported, so never reveal who made a report.
  • Child-safety reports never come to server moderators. They go straight to the Hearth safety team, which handles the legal reporting. If you see anything involving the sexual exploitation of a minor, don't screenshot or share it. Report it with the Child sexual abuse or exploitation category right away.

7. Audit log

⚙ → Audit log lists who did what and why for 90 days: kicks, bans, timeouts, role and channel changes, permission changes, deleted messages, purges and report decisions. Use it to keep your mod team accountable and to settle disputes.

9. Ownership and deleting

  • Transfer ownership: ⚙ → Overview → Transfer ownership, pick a member and enter your password. You stay a member.
  • Delete the server: ⚙ → Overview → Delete server, then type the server's name. All channels, messages, roles and files disappear for everyone immediately and can't be restored.
  • What "deleted" means: deleted content is gone from Hearth right away. For safety and legal reasons, an encrypted copy is kept for 30 days, reachable only by the Hearth safety team, then erased for good. Reported content is kept for 1 year.
  • You must delete or transfer your servers before you can delete your own account.

10. Your responsibilities

Running a server means following the Terms of Service and keeping illegal content out. In particular:

  • Content involving the sexual exploitation of minors is never allowed anywhere. Report it, don't share it.
  • Adult content belongs only in 18+ channels or servers, and only involving consenting adults.
  • Threats, doxxing, trafficking, illegal sales and scams are removed and can lead to the whole server being deleted.
  • Your server rules can be stricter than Hearth's, but never looser.

11. Hearth admins (site operators)

The person who runs this Hearth (the first account, plus anyone they promote) has a 🛡 Hearth admin button on the far left. It covers the whole site, not one server:

  • Reports: child-safety reports (always listed first), reports about direct messages and whole servers, and anything members also sent to Hearth. Actions: dismiss, remove the message, suspend the account, delete the server.
  • Child safety (legal duty): US law (18 U.S.C. 2258A) requires reporting apparent child sexual abuse material to NCMEC's CyberTipline (report.cybertip.org) as soon as reasonably possible. File the report, record its number in the report, and don't download or share the material. Evidence is preserved for 1 year automatically.
  • Users: search accounts, suspend or restore them (suspension signs them out everywhere), reveal an email address for a legal reason, reset a wrongly entered birth date, and grant or remove admin.
  • Legal holds: when you receive a preservation request from law enforcement or expect legal action, place a hold on the user or server. Their data won't be erased until you release it.
  • Retention vault: encrypted copies of deleted content (30 days) and report evidence (1 year). Opening an item requires a reason.
  • Admin log: every admin action and every time protected data is opened, with the reason given. Admins are accountable too.

Legal questions about a specific situation (subpoenas, court orders, emergency requests) should go to a lawyer.

12. Moving a server from Discord

Tap + in the server list → Move a server from Discord. The wizard walks you through it. In short:

  1. Create a bot in the Discord Developer Portal, turn on Message Content Intent, and copy its token. Hearth keeps the token only in memory while copying and never saves it.
  2. Add the bot to your Discord server with the invite link the wizard gives you (it can only view channels and read history). For private channels, give the bot access to them, or give it Administrator just for the move.
  3. Choose what comes over. Every channel is created; for each text channel, choose whether to Bring messages. Keep history for useful channels (rules, guides) and start noisy ones blank. You can also skip files or only bring messages after a date.
  4. Confirm and start. You can close the window; it keeps going. If Hearth restarts mid-way, open the wizard again and paste the token to continue where it stopped.

What transfers

  • Server name and icon; categories, text and voice channels in order, with topics, slowmode and 18+ flags.
  • Roles with their names, colors, order and the matching Hearth permissions. Roles that belong to Discord bots are skipped. If @everyone had Administrator on Discord, it's left off here for safety.
  • Role permissions on each channel (private and read-only channels stay that way).
  • Chat history for the channels you picked: text, replies, files, embeds (as text), and reaction counts. Mentions become plain names.

What doesn't

  • Members. People join with an invite and start fresh. Old messages keep the sender's name and picture with an ARCHIVED tag, but they belong to no Hearth account, even if someone with the same name joins later. Nobody can edit or claim them; moderators can delete them.
  • Bots and their commands, webhooks, threads, forum and media channels, pins, custom emoji (shown as :name:), per-person channel permissions, and DMs.

After the move

  1. Imported channels start hidden from everyone except you and Administrators, marked HIDDEN. Use Review channels at the top of the channel list: check the roles, set each channel's permissions, then Open it (or Open all).
  2. Retire the bot: reset its token in the Developer Portal (or delete the app) and remove it from your Discord server.
  3. Invite people and give them roles as they arrive.

You're responsible for imported content like anything else in your server. Only move servers you own or run, and let your members know their messages are being copied.
